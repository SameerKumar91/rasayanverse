Buffer Solution & pH

Observe how a buffer resists large pH changes when small amounts of acid or base are added.

⚗ Solutions • pH⏱ 10–12 min🎓 Guided Practical
Reaction / Principle
Buffer + H⁺/OH⁻ → Reduced pH Change
A buffer contains a weak acid/base pair that consumes added H⁺ or OH⁻.
ConceptAimApparatusChemicalsSafetyProcedure🧪 Virtual LabResultQuiz
01 • BASIC CONCEPT

What is a Buffer Solution?

A buffer solution resists large changes in pH when small amounts of acid or base are added.

A common buffer contains a weak acid and its conjugate base. The pair reacts with added hydrogen or hydroxide ions.

In this virtual practical, you will compare the pH before and after small additions of acid or base.

pH = pKₐ + log([A⁻]/[HA])

This principle is the key idea behind the practical.

🎯
AIM

To study the resistance of a buffer solution to changes in pH after adding small amounts of acid or base.

WHAT HAPPENS?

Why does a buffer resist pH change?

The weak acid component can react with added OH⁻ while the conjugate base can react with added H⁺. This consumes much of the added acid or base and limits the pH change.
