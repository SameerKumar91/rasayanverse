PRACTICAL 01

Acid–Base Titration

Determine the concentration of an unknown acid solution by reacting it with a standard base solution until the endpoint is reached.

⚗ Acid • Base ⏱ 10–15 min 🎓 Guided Practical
Reaction
HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O
Acid + Base → Salt + Water
Concept Aim Apparatus Chemicals Safety Procedure 🧪 Virtual Lab Result Quiz
01 • BASIC CONCEPT

What is Acid–Base Titration?

Titration is a laboratory method used to determine the concentration of an unknown solution by reacting it with a solution whose concentration is already known.

In this experiment, sodium hydroxide (NaOH) is added slowly from a burette to hydrochloric acid (HCl). A small amount of phenolphthalein is used as an indicator.

Near the endpoint, the solution changes from colourless to a very faint pink colour. This tells us that the acid has been neutralized.

HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O

This is a neutralization reaction between an acid and a base.

🎯
AIM

To determine the concentration of an unknown hydrochloric acid solution using standard sodium hydroxide solution.

06 • PROCEDURE

Follow the Experiment

Read each step before performing the corresponding action in the virtual laboratory.

01

Prepare the burette

Rinse and fill the burette with standard 0.1 M NaOH solution.

02

Take the acid sample

Transfer a measured volume of HCl into a clean conical flask.

03

Add indicator

Add a few drops of phenolphthalein to the acid solution.

04

Start titration

Slowly add NaOH from the burette while gently swirling the flask.

05

Find the endpoint

Stop when a faint pink colour persists. Record the burette reading.

WHAT HAPPENS?

Why does the colour change?

Phenolphthalein is colourless in an acidic solution. As NaOH neutralizes the HCl, the solution gradually moves toward the basic range. At the endpoint, a very small excess of NaOH makes phenolphthalein turn faint pink.
